## Course overview

In ECSE 2660 at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute, I worked across operating-system behavior, assembly programming, and digital logic. The course projects connected source-level programs to process execution, register use, procedure calls, and circuit state.

## Processes and instruction-level experiments

I developed multiprocess C programs with fork and exec to examine process creation and race conditions. I also implemented inline x86 assembly variants of a Collatz computation to investigate instruction-level trade-offs. These exercises concern program and process behavior rather than a processor-pipeline simulator.

## Assembly and digital logic

I wrote modular MIPS assembly involving IEEE 754 floating-point arithmetic and calculator logic, managing stack frames, saved registers, and procedure calls according to MIPS calling conventions.

In LogicWorks, I designed and simulated combinational and sequential circuits and checked their logic functions, state behavior, and datapath components.
