This page summarizes the core experimental skills and circuit techniques developed through the hands-on laboratories in ECSE 2050 Introduction to Electronics. These exercises provided a practical foundation in analog behavior, components, circuit analysis and electrical measurement.

## Experimental Skills and Capabilities

- Constructing circuits on breadboards using resistors, capacitors, diodes, BJTs and op-amps  
- Characterizing diode I–V curves and understanding rectification behavior  
- Building and testing half-wave and full-wave rectifiers with filtering  
- Designing RC filters and measuring frequency response using oscilloscopes  
- Implementing op-amp stages including inverting, non-inverting, summing and buffer configurations  
- Designing active filters and analyzing gain, cutoff frequency and stability  
- Performing small-signal and large-signal measurements for amplifier circuits  
- Debugging circuits using multimeters, oscilloscopes and function generators  
- Interpreting Bode plots and comparing measured responses to theoretical predictions  
- Understanding Zener diode regulation and voltage clamping behavior  

## Concepts Covered

- Diode conduction and rectification  
- Zener regulation and clipping  
- BJTs in switching and amplification  
- Operational amplifier behavior and gain design  
- Active and passive filters  
- Frequency response and Bode analysis  
- Noise, biasing and stability considerations  

## Outcome

These experiments provided foundational proficiency in analog circuit construction, measurement and debugging. The laboratory experience supported later coursework in advanced hardware design, digital systems, embedded systems and power electronics.
